How to Compare Egg Buyers and Selling Costs in Uganda

The highest quoted tray price is not always the best sale. Transport, breakage, delayed payment, rejected eggs, packaging and the time spent delivering can reduce the amount that remains with the farmer.

Unified Farm BLM raises local chickens. The market figures below are dated examples for comparison, not our current production, sales volume or guaranteed prices. Confirm current quotations directly with buyers before making a plan.

Reduce Dependence on One Buyer

Depending on one buyer creates concentration risk: a closure, delayed payment or change in specifications can interrupt sales. A second realistic route may help, but only if the farm can serve both without overpromising supply. Write down each buyer's likely quantity, payment timing, quality requirements and transport cost.

Questions for a First Buyer Visit

  • Egg type and size: does the buyer want local-chicken eggs, commercial eggs, mixed sizes or a declared weight grade?
  • Quantity: what is the minimum useful delivery, and is that quantity an estimate or a confirmed order?
  • Acceptance: how are cracks, heavy soiling, dates, packaging and rejected trays handled?
  • Payment: is payment immediate, weekly, monthly or after resale, and who approves it?
  • Delivery: which day, location and receiving hours apply, and who pays transport?
  • Trial: can both sides test a small quantity before discussing a regular arrangement?
Do not promise unverified supply: state what is available now, explain that local-hen production can vary, and confirm each collection or delivery before the buyer makes plans.

Track Seasonality Instead of Assuming It

Demand may change during school terms, holidays, religious seasons or local events, but the direction and size of the change are not guaranteed. Record quotations from the same buyer categories every month. After several months, your own dated record is more useful than a general claim about when prices rise or fall.

Describe the Product Honestly

Record the collection period, egg type, quantity, grading method and shell condition. Do not improvise washing or cleaning practices: follow applicable food-safety and buyer requirements. Our egg handling guide explains the questions to resolve.

When you are ready to approach shops or schools, keep simple records of who bought what and who owes you. A paper sales book, spreadsheet or a suitable bookkeeping app can work; the important point is to record each transaction consistently.

Start with a short list of nearby buyers and collect information before offering a price. A buyer who wants a small regular quantity may be more suitable than a distant buyer quoting a higher headline price.

Calculate the Net Price, Not Just the Tray Price

Use the following calculation for each route:

Net amount per tray = selling price − packaging − transport allocation − expected breakage or rejection cost − collection or payment cost.

If one boda trip costs UGX 6,000 and carries three trays, the transport allocation is UGX 2,000 per tray. If the same safe trip carries ten trays, it is UGX 600 per tray. Do not overload a vehicle or compromise safety to reduce this number.

Credit also has a cost. A higher price paid after thirty days may be less useful than a slightly lower price paid immediately, especially when feed must be purchased before payment arrives.

Use a Seven-Day Market Check

  1. List at least three buyer categories within a realistic transport area.
  2. Ask each buyer the same questions about size, quantity, payment, rejection and delivery.
  3. Record the person's name or role, date and whether the quotation is firm or informal.
  4. Calculate the net amount per tray after serving that buyer.
  5. Offer only a quantity the farm can confirm.
  6. Review the result after the first transaction before discussing regular supply.

Payment and Order Safety

  • Confirm the order, total price, delivery point and payment timing in writing where possible.
  • Issue a simple receipt showing the quantity, date and amount received.
  • Record credit separately from cash; an unpaid invoice is not available money.
  • Avoid sending sensitive identity, banking or account information to an unverified contact.
  • For a new buyer, keep the first transaction small enough that a delay or rejection will not threaten the farm.

What to Record After Every Sale

Write the buyer, date, quantity accepted, quantity rejected, unit price, transport cost, amount paid, amount outstanding and the next agreed action. These records show which buyer is actually valuable over time.

Sources and limitations

This article is a market-comparison worksheet, not a live Uganda price service or a promise of demand. Prices, food-safety rules and institutional purchasing requirements can change. Verify them before each arrangement.
