Where to Sell Your Eggs for the Best Price in Uganda (And How to Keep Those Buyers)

Two farmers in the same village sell the same tray of eggs. One gets UGX 8,000. The other gets UGX 11,000 — every week, reliably, with buyers calling her before the eggs are even collected. The difference is not luck and it is not the eggs. It is choosing markets deliberately instead of taking whoever shows up first.

What Each Market Actually Pays

MarketTypical tray priceThe Reality
Farm gate (neighbours)UGX 8,000–9,000Easy, zero transport, but limited volume
Local shops & kiosksUGX 9,000–10,000Steady weekly orders; they resell at 10–12k
Schools & institutionsUGX 9,500–11,000Big volumes, formal — may pay monthly
Restaurants & chapati makersUGX 10,000–12,000Daily demand, quality-obsessed
Town markets / brokersUGX 8,500–10,000They come to you but squeeze prices
Direct to homes (delivery)UGX 11,000–13,000Best price, but costs you time

Prices vary by district and season — these are typical Mpigi/Wakiso/Kampala figures in mid-2026.

The Two-Buyer Rule

Never depend on one buyer. The farmer whose only customer is one shop is one quarrel away from a store full of unsold eggs — and eggs wait for nobody. Aim for at least two buyers in different categories: say a school (bulk, weekly) plus three chapati makers (daily, small). If one stumbles, the other absorbs the eggs.

How to Win a Buyer in One Visit

  • Carry a sample tray — clean, uniform eggs. First impressions are physical.
  • Offer consistency, not just eggs: "I can deliver two trays every Tuesday and Friday without fail." Reliability is what shops actually buy.
  • Start slightly under market for the first month (UGX 500 less), then normalize. It buys you the trial period.
  • Take back cracked eggs without argument. It costs you one egg and buys permanent trust.
The secret of the UGX 11,000 farmer: she delivers the same day, same time, every week, and sends a WhatsApp message the evening before: "Tomorrow I bring 4 trays." Buyers plan around her. She is not a supplier anymore — she is infrastructure.

Timing: When Eggs Are Gold

Egg prices in Uganda are not flat. They climb during school terms (institutions buying), December festivities, and fasting seasons. They dip in January (everyone is broke) and long school holidays. Smart farmers expand the flock so peak laying meets peak price — and use the cheap months to build new buyer relationships, because farmers who disappear in the dip lose their shelf space to someone else.

Add Value Before You Add Customers

Before hunting for new buyers, squeeze more from the eggs you have: grade them (uniform trays sell faster), clean them properly (never wash with cold water — it pulls bacteria through the shell), and pack neatly. A presentable tray at a fair price beats a cheap ugly tray every time. Our egg freshness and handling guide covers the details.

And when you are ready to supply shops and schools properly, keep simple records of who bought what and who owes you — chasing payments from memory is how small farms bleed. We use DukaBooks, our own free app, to track every tray and every debt. Whatever tool you use, write it down.

Start this week: list five potential buyers within boda distance, visit two, offer the trial. Your future loyal customer is probably frying chapatis right now, annoyed at their unreliable egg guy.
