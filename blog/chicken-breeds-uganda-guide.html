Chicken Types in Uganda: Local and Commercial Comparison

Choosing a chicken type affects housing, feed, health planning, time to market and the buyers you need. Unified Farm BLM mainly raises local chickens; the commercial and improved types below are included for educational comparison using published agricultural guidance and market examples, not as breeds raised on our farm.

How to read the figures: Performance numbers are breeder targets or broad examples, while UGX prices are market examples rather than current quotations. Results depend on the particular line, source, feed, housing, health and management. Confirm both the bird identity and current price before buying.

Detailed Breed Profiles

Best for Beginners

Kuroiler (Keggfarms, India)

Kuroiler is an improved dual-purpose type developed by Keggfarms. Published Keggfarms material describes performance under semi-scavenging conditions, but those targets should not be treated as guaranteed results in Uganda.

  • Growth: Males reach 3.5–4 kg in 5 months; females reach 2.5–3 kg
  • Eggs: 150–200 brown eggs per year
  • Feeding: May be managed in semi-scavenging systems, but still needs clean water and a nutritionally adequate ration
  • Health: Management claims from the supplier do not replace vaccination planning, biosecurity or veterinary care
  • Price: UGX 3,000–4,500 per day-old chick
Comparison note: Kuroilers are an improved dual-purpose type, not part of our current flock. Farmers considering them should confirm chick source, management needs and current performance information with a reputable hatchery or agricultural adviser.
Best for Egg Production

ISA Brown (France)

ISA Brown is a commercial brown-egg layer. The breeder publishes management and performance targets; actual output depends on following an appropriate feeding, lighting, housing and flock-health programme.

  • Growth: Lightweight birds (1.8–2.2 kg), not ideal for meat
  • Eggs: 300–320 large brown eggs per year
  • Feed efficiency: Requires 110–120g of layer feed daily
  • Hardiness: Moderate; susceptible to heat stress and disease
  • Price: UGX 4,500–6,000 per day-old chick
Important: Do not assume a headline egg figure will be achieved under every system. Read the current supplier guide and check whether your housing, feed, water, climate control and health support can meet its requirements.
Best for Meat

Cobb 500 Broiler (USA)

Cobb 500 is a commercial broiler line. Cobb publishes performance objectives under defined management conditions; they are planning targets, not a promise that every flock will reach a stated weight by a stated day.

  • Growth: 2.0–2.5 kg in 6–7 weeks
  • Eggs: Poor layer; not kept for eggs
  • Feed efficiency: 1.6–1.8 kg feed per 1 kg of weight gain (excellent)
  • Hardiness: Low; needs temperature-controlled housing and strict biosecurity
  • Price: UGX 3,500–4,500 per day-old chick
Best for Free-Range

Local Village Chicken (Indigenous)

Local or indigenous chickens are the type raised at Unified Farm BLM. They are often kept in scavenging or semi-scavenging systems, but they still need safe housing, clean water, adequate feed and health care. Local birds can also become sick and suffer losses.

  • Growth: Slow — 6–9 months to reach 1.5–2 kg
  • Eggs: Output varies widely with the hen, season, nutrition, health and brooding pattern
  • Feeding: Scavenging may contribute to the diet but does not guarantee adequate nutrition
  • Health: Local birds may be relatively adaptable, but they are not disease-proof
  • Price: UGX 1,000–2,500 per chick; mature birds sell for UGX 25,000–40,000
Market note: Some buyers prefer local chicken, but price premiums vary by location, bird size, season and buyer. Obtain quotations from several likely buyers before budgeting.

Which Breed Should You Choose?

Your Situation Recommended Breed Why
Total beginner, limited budget Kuroiler or Local Village Hardy, low input, forgiving of mistakes
Goal: maximum eggs ISA Brown 300+ eggs/year with proper care
Goal: fast meat production Cobb 500 or Ross 308 2 kg in 6–7 weeks
Free-range, low-input system Kuroiler, Sasso, or Local Can scavenge and tolerate harsh conditions
Mixed meat + eggs Rainbow Rooster or Kuroiler Good balance of both products
Premium meat market Local Village or Sasso Consumers pay premium for taste and quality

How to Check a Chick Supplier

  • Ask for the exact bird type or commercial line and written hatch date.
  • Request the vaccination and handling information for that batch.
  • Confirm collection arrangements, replacement terms and who answers health-history questions.
  • Use current contacts from the supplier itself or ask a district veterinary or agricultural office how to verify a seller.
Buying tip: Inspect chicks before accepting them and record any deaths, weakness, wetness, pasted vents or transport problems immediately. If you are unsure about the batch, seek qualified veterinary advice instead of relying on an arbitrary percentage rule.

Sources and limitations
