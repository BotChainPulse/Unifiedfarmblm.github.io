Backyard & Small-Flock Chicken Farming: Start Here

A practical route through housing, feeding, health planning and selling for backyard keepers and small-scale farmers worldwide.

Our farm context: Unified Farm BLM mainly raises indigenous/local chickens in Mpigi. Commercial layers, broilers and improved breeds in our other guides are educational comparisons, not our current flock or farm results.

Readers outside Uganda: the planning process can be useful elsewhere, but UGX budgets, local markets and breed examples describe the Ugandan context. Use current prices, suitable housing for your climate and veterinary guidance from your own area.

Choose your next step

A small flock needs more than birds. Before buying, decide what you want to produce, where the birds will live, how you will pay for feed and who might buy your products. Use the route below to work through these decisions in order. The linked guides explain each topic in more detail; the calculators use your own figures.

1. Plan before buying

Decide whether your priority is household food, eggs, breeding or selling birds. Write down the money and daily time you can commit, then price the essentials locally.

Ask potential buyers about quantity, packaging, collection and payment. Compare what you keep after transport and losses, rather than choosing a buyer by the advertised price alone.

What to ask before buying local chickens

  • What is the bird's age and source, and how is the chicken type identified?
  • What feed has it been receiving, and what changes will it need after moving?
  • What vaccination or treatment records are available?
  • What quantity is available now, and what is included in the quoted price?
  • How will collection or transport be arranged?

Write the answers down. If the seller cannot confirm a detail, treat it as unknown when planning. A photograph alone cannot verify breed, age, health or expected production.

Use current prices, not fixed profit promises

Feed, bird and transport prices vary by supplier and date. Get quotations for the same quantities and compare complete costs. Our starter budgets are worked examples; they do not promise that your flock will cost the same or earn a fixed income.

For a feed estimate, enter bird count, daily intake, planning days and wastage in the feed quantity planner. For eggs, use your own laying records and include unsold or damaged eggs in the egg margin calculation. Revisit the estimate after recording actual results.

Questions about local-chicken farming

Can local chickens use the commercial-layer figures in your guides?

Do not treat those figures as a prediction for local hens. Our commercial comparisons describe different production systems. Use observed flock records and ask for advice appropriate to your birds.

Where can I ask about Unified Farm BLM products?

Contact Unified Farm BLM with the item, quantity and your location. Availability, price, health records and collection arrangements must be confirmed before you plan a purchase.

What should I do if birds are sick?

Contact a qualified veterinary professional. Our poultry health guide helps you prepare observations and questions; it cannot diagnose your flock.
